## How the engine keeps its promises

- **One piece of due work per tick**, worked out from each prospect's stage and next-action time. Nothing is queued in a table, so nothing queued can outlive a stop.
- **The claim.** A step is sent only after one database transaction moves its ledger row from planned to in progress, freezes the text and records that the person was contacted. The database then refuses a second send of that step.
- **Unknown results are checked on LinkedIn, never retried blindly.** After a crash or an error mid-send, the engine reads the profile or the conversation before it does anything else for that person.
- **Before every message** it reads the conversation. A reply, or a message you wrote yourself, stops the sequence. The send carries what the conversation looked like and a deadline; the driver refuses if either no longer holds.
- **Limits and hours** are asked when work is picked and again right before the claim, with the settings as they are at that moment.
- **A safety stop** (login wall, checkpoint, restriction, LinkedIn's own invitation limit, three unrecognised pages in a row) halts everything until you resume. It is stored in its own table and survives a restart.

### Does the simulation notice a broken engine?

I broke the real engine on purpose and ran 2 seeds each time. This was done on the version before the last round of fixes; the simulation has only become stricter since.

| Break | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Unknown messages retried without checking LinkedIn | No double send (the conversation check before the send caught each one), and 3 violations of "the app's records match what LinkedIn received" |
| The above, and the manual-message check switched off | 53 violations: 18 double sends, 27 messages after a reply or a manual message |
| Working window ignored | Every tick failed and was reported; nothing was sent |

The simulation's own tests also run it against small deliberately wrong engines: double invite after a crash, ignores the window, ignores the daily limit, messages after a reply, invites a suppressed person, pays off a backlog after downtime, never takes a break, forgets its break count on restart, ignores the send deadline, does nothing. Each is reported by the assertion meant for it.

### A real crash, not a simulated one

`src/engine/sim/sigkill.test.ts` starts a child process that commits one row and opens a second transaction, sends it SIGKILL, and reopens the file. The committed row is there, the uncommitted one is not, and the integrity check passes.

## What the simulation and the reviews found

Found by the simulation in the first version of the engine, and fixed:

- After a crash the restarted engine could load a page 41 s after the previous one; the minimum gap is 45 s.
- A page read interrupted by a crash stayed "unknown" in the ledger for ever.
- After a crash right after a message went out, a hand-written message from you left the person in "needs review" with the send recorded as unknown. Nothing wrong was sent; the record was wrong.

Found by the reviewers and fixed (decisions 45–61):

- A send after closing time when the laptop sleeps during the read before it, or inside the send itself.
- A reply arriving between the conversation read and the send. The simulation's assertion had been forgiving it; that is removed.
- An old unknown invite retried after you had withdrawn it by hand.
- A step retried without limit.
- A person held for review, or a failed tick, when you pause a campaign or move a prospect while the engine is working on them.
- A limit lowered or a template edited during a read being ignored for that one action.
- A crash loop that never takes a break.
- Two overlapping ticks sending the same step.
- A missed acceptance never found when withdrawals are set to 0.

## Known limits, recorded as decisions

- **Invite note typed again by hand (51).** If LinkedIn does not show the invite note in the conversation and you send the identical text yourself, the check reads it as the note. Whether LinkedIn shows the note can only be seen on the live site; milestone 4 makes the rule exact.
- **Working hours at 2 a.m. on the October clock change (62)** can open up to an hour early on that one night. Daytime hours are not affected.
- **One process only (54).** Two engines on one database would each treat the other's send as interrupted. The app's port and the Chrome profile lock already stop a second app; milestone 3 must hold both before the first tick.
- **An instant remains on the real site** between the driver's last look at the conversation and its click.
- **A retried step counts once in its budget (35, 48).** It is retried only after LinkedIn showed the first attempt did not go out, at most three times.

## Not verified

- Anything on the live site. The write operations (invite, message, withdraw) exist only in the simulated LinkedIn until milestone 4. Decision 63 lists three things the simulation cannot answer: whether LinkedIn shows the invite note in the conversation, how it redraws a message that contains a link, and that re-inviting is blocked after a withdrawal.
- Whether every real profile shows a member id. The engine holds a person for review when the profile page shows none, and the live parser reads it from the top card's Message link. Milestone 3's live discovery run will show how often it is missing.
- Power loss on real hardware. The database syncs every commit to the drive, and a test asserts the settings; the crash test above is a killed process, not a pulled plug.
- The last working day of each simulated run has no crashes or faults, so that an unknown result from its final minutes is not left unchecked when the run ends.
- The deliberate breaks of the real engine were not repeated on the final code.
- No screenshots: this milestone changed nothing on screen.
